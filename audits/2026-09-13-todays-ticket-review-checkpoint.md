## What went wrong

### 1. Target-odds contamination was a direct process breach

The report stated that weak selections should not be added merely to reach a headline price, then called Napoli, Juventus, Frankfurt and Boston “price-building selections” and used acknowledged thin-data basketball and regulation-only baseball to manufacture 1,000× and 10,000× totals. This is preventable and independent of results.

Resolution: admission standards never relax for a requested combined-odds target. If the qualified pool cannot reach the target, report that it is analytically unattainable. A synthetic lottery illustration is not a recommendation and is excluded from performance reporting.

### 2. “Independent tickets” were not independent portfolios

Sporting, Spain and USA appeared in all four tickets. The three headline tickets shared seven exact legs: Sporting, Vancouver, Flamengo, Spain, USA, Detroit and Milwaukee.

With equal stakes:

- Sporting, Spain and USA had 100% portfolio exposure.
- Vancouver, Flamengo, Detroit and Milwaukee had 75% exposure.
- 104.07 ↔ 1,037.92 shared 10 legs; Jaccard overlap 0.417.
- 104.07 ↔ 10,501.22 shared 7 legs; Jaccard overlap 0.250.
- 1,037.92 ↔ 10,501.22 shared 11 legs; Jaccard overlap 0.423.

Sporting defeated every ticket; Milwaukee defeated every headline ticket. The constructions were separate slips but not diversified risk.

Resolution: “independent” means disjoint fixtures/selections. Otherwise label tickets overlapping variants and publish a leg-by-ticket exposure matrix.

### 3. Plausibility was mistaken for value

Grades such as A- and B+ had no defined quantitative rubric. No leg recorded a calibrated probability estimate, uncertainty interval, de-vigged consensus baseline or expected value.

Benfica at 1.14 required an 87.7% offered-odds break-even probability. A winning streak and a predicted score do not establish 87.7%. Benfica won, but the process remained incomplete. Hammarby also won despite the report itself citing an external estimate near 63.5% against an 82.6% break-even price; the winning outcome does not rehabilitate a negative-edge decision.

Resolution: every leg must record odds/time, reciprocal break-even, de-vigged market baseline, estimated probability and interval, conservative edge, evidence rubric and exact settlement mechanics.

### 4. Draw risk was named but not modeled

Four of the five settled football losses were draws. Sporting’s 1.55 price corresponds to a 64.5% break-even benchmark; a 1–1 draw is ordinary outcome variance, but the A- label was unjustified because no numerical draw probability or conservative edge was documented. Krasnodar, Celta and Lens were worse: they lacked a fixture-specific evidence block and functioned as price builders.

Resolution: every 1X2 favourite requires an explicit draw probability. Compare 1X2 with draw-no-bet and double chance on price and expected value; do not mechanically soften the market if the re-priced alternative also lacks edge.

### 5. Known warnings did not become exclusion gates

The report acknowledged Flamengo rotation, a competitive USA–France final, an unconfirmed Dodgers starter, lower-tier basketball uncertainty and the added fragility of regulation-only baseball. Most were still retained unchanged.

The Dodgers’ late 6–4 loss was game variance, but recommending a regulation result with known starter uncertainty was preventable. Milwaukee’s rationale leaned heavily on 33 runs in the preceding two games; the subsequent 4–3 loss highlights recency bias and missing bullpen/pitcher analysis.

Resolution: a material caveat must trigger exclusion or a quantified downgrade. Warnings cannot coexist with an unchanged principal recommendation.

### 6. Long-accumulator fragility was knowingly reintroduced

The 16-, 18- and 19-leg tickets contradicted the existing five-leg analytical ceiling. Their offered-odds break-even benchmarks were 0.96%, 0.096% and 0.0095%; corresponding failure benchmarks were 99.04%, 99.904% and 99.9905%. These are not forecasts, but they correctly show the payout hurdle.

Resolution: default to two or three legs; hard analytical ceiling five. Six or more is entertainment-only and must not be called a high-confidence or evidence-led recommendation.

### 7. Thin-data basketball failed exactly where warned

Spain and USA won. Frankfurt also won, but one winner does not validate an unsupported price-building choice. The three lower-tier basketball additions in the 10,501.22 ticket lost 0–3. Balıkesir’s one-point loss contains variance, but inclusion without verified lineups and calibrated pricing remained a process failure.

Resolution: exclude preseason, friendly and thin-data club basketball unless active rosters, rotations and a calibrated price edge are verified.

## Outcome-versus-process classification

- Irreducible variance with incomplete calibration: Sporting draw; Balıkesir one-point loss.
- Probability/value error: undocumented short-priced favourites and Hammarby retained despite cited negative edge.
- Price-target error: Juventus, Krasnodar, Celta, Lens, Frankfurt, Boston and lower-tier basketball used to build odds.
- Portfolio/correlation error: repeated Sporting, Milwaukee and five other shared legs.
- Lineup/news gate error: Flamengo and Dodgers retained despite material uncertainty.
- Market-definition/risk error: regulation-only MLB selections used without an estimated nine-inning tie probability.
