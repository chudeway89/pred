## Corrections to carry forward
1. Record the exact fixture date, team identity, market wording, line, odds, source and verification status before endorsement.
2. An OR market wins when either condition succeeds. Hansa losing 0–4 does not lose “Hansa OR over 2.5”.
3. Win-either-half settlement requires half-time and full-time information, not just the final score.
4. Do not automatically interpret an integer total or handicap as Asian. Check the exact product rules. Asian over 3 normally pushes at three; a different product may not.
5. Early-payout 1UP/2UP products are not generally identical to “must lead by one/two” bets. Verify the actual terms before judging.
6. Reciprocal decimal odds are a raw break-even benchmark, not a calibrated true probability. Pushes, correlation, margin and promotion rules complicate ticket-level interpretation.
7. Positive model edge against a de-vigged market share does not alone imply positive expected return at the offered price.
8. A favourite, positive form or lower line can be plausible without offering value. Do not invent probability estimates.
9. Do not restore rejected or unresearched selections merely to reach 100/1,000/10,000 odds.
10. One loss does not prove a whole league or market is bad. No new permanent league blacklist follows from this small sample.
11. Keep confirmed, provisional and unresolved outcomes separate. Never infer full settlement from a homepage snippet.
12. Check audit completeness against every original leg; do not describe a partial list as a complete audit.
13. Multi-agent review adds independent checks, not the credentials or certainty of licensed professional analysts. Research budget and coverage limitations must be disclosed.
