The 4.01 core was the only winning ticket. It contained Ajax–Willem II Asian over 3, Savoia +1 at Foggia, Sorrento +1 at Cavese, Drochtersen/Assel–Kickers Emden over 2.5, and West Ham–Fulham over 1.5.

The 17.30 expansion failed on Grasshoppers–Sion over 8.5 corners: only six corners were recorded. Al Hilal–Al Gharafa Asian over 3 pushed at exactly three goals.

The 106.64 ticket failed on Middlesbrough to beat Millwall (0–0) and Hibernian to beat Kilmarnock (0–1). Fiorentina–Pisa Asian over 3 pushed at 3–0.

The 1,038.43 ticket inherited those failures and added Linfield to beat Portadown (1–1), Carlisle–Forest Green Asian over 3 (1–1), and Al Nassr to beat Al Ain (Al Ain won 4–0).

## Process review

- A 65% HAEQW3 raw win rate did not make the 40-leg ticket viable. Flexi protection covering one error was structurally mismatched to nine losses.
- The 4.01 five-leg core won, but one successful ticket is not proof of calibration.
- The 17.30 expansion repeated the prohibited practice of adding a fragile corner leg to increase price.
- Nordsjælland–Midtjylland was a women's fixture, but some pre-match support came from men's-team data. The leg won 4–1; the process was nevertheless invalid.
- The 106.64 and 1,038.43 tickets were nested, not independent. The larger ticket inherited every risk in the smaller ticket.
- Short-priced football favourites still need draw/upset modelling. Middlesbrough, Hibernian and Linfield failed through a draw or defeat.
- Asian totals are win–push–loss contracts. Fiorentina–Pisa over 3 and Al Hilal–Al Gharafa over 3 were pushes, not predictive wins.
- Cup football markets were settled at 90 minutes unless the saved market explicitly stated qualification.
- Conflicting aggregator metadata for Helsinki and Drochtersen showed why exact event IDs, competition, sex/age class and timestamps are mandatory.
